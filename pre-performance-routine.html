WHAT WE NOTICE

ROUTINES CAN DRIFT OVER TIME.

Athletes talk about pre-performance routines a lot, and that’s a great start. What we sometimes notice is a routine that has slowly become something else.

  • 01

    A LUCKY RITUAL

    Kept because it went well once, rather than because it helps you focus.

  • 02

    A LONG CHECKLIST

    So many steps that it becomes one more thing to get right.

  • 03

    A WAY TO FEEL CALM

    Measured by whether the nerves went away.

  • 04

    GAME DAY ONLY

    Saved for big moments, but rarely practised at training.

THE EVIDENCE

DOES IT HELP? THE RESEARCH SAYS YES.

A meta-analysis brought together 112 results from 33 studies. Athletes who used a routine performed better than those who didn’t, and the difference was moderate to large.

  • 33 STUDIES pooled in one meta-analysis
  • 15 SPORTS golf, basketball, tennis, football and more
  • AGES 11 TO 40 beginners through to elite
  • ONE PIECE simple routines worked as well as longer ones

Worth knowing: the pressure findings are less precise, with fewer studies and only five in real competition.

Rupprecht, Tran & Gröpel (2024), International Review of Sport and Exercise Psychology, https://doi.org/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1944271 (opens in a new tab)

THE KEY IDEA

A ROUTINE IS AN ANCHOR, NOT AN OFF SWITCH.

Before an important moment, most athletes notice some mental noise: thoughts like “don’t miss this”, nerves, a racing heart, the crowd. If your routine’s job is to make that noise disappear, every nervous moment feels like the routine has failed, and the noise stays centre stage. If its job is to give your attention somewhere to come back to, the noise can stay and you can still commit to the skill. Over time, many athletes notice the noise has less grip.

AN ANCHOR DOESN’T STOP THE WAVES. IT STOPS YOU DRIFTING.

THE PROCESS

BUILD YOURS IN FIVE STEPS.

  1. 1

    CHOOSE YOUR MOMENT

    One skill where you decide when to start: a shot at goal, a serve, a free throw, a putt.

  2. 2

    FIND YOUR FOCUS

    The one thing your attention needs to land on, in one or two words.

  3. 3

    PICK ONE TO THREE PIECES

    A breath, a picture of the result, a cue word, eyes on the target, a set move or a countdown.

  4. 4

    PUT IT IN ORDER AND TIME IT

    Same sequence and rhythm each time, plus a short version for when you’re rushed.

  5. 5

    PRACTISE IT WITH THE NOISE

    Calmly at first, then with a little pressure, so it’s familiar before you need it.

The full guide walks through each step, with examples from six sports, troubleshooting tips and a printable routine planner.

QUESTIONS

Is the kōrero really free?

Yes. It’s a free 10 to 15 minute conversation, with no commitment and no obligation.

Does the kōrero have to be about my routine?

No. It’s a chance to talk about whatever’s going on for you in your sport, whether that’s nerves, confidence, motivation, coming back from injury or something else. We’ll listen, and we can go from there.

Do I need to be an elite athlete?

No. We work with athletes at every level, and the research on routines found benefits from beginners right through to elite.

Can my parent or caregiver be involved?

Yes. For younger athletes, it’s common for a parent or caregiver to get in touch and be part of the conversation.

Who will I be talking to?

One of our team of registered psychologists working in sport.

Is this therapy?

Our work focuses on the mental side of sport and performance. If something bigger is going on, we’ll talk honestly about what kind of support would fit best.

Where are you based?

We’re based in Ōtautahi Christchurch and work with athletes across the motu, in person and online.

Can I share the guide?

Yes, please do. Send it to teammates, your coach or whānau. It’s free, and there’s no sign-up.

Do I have to read the guide first?

Not at all. You can book a kōrero, download the guide, or both.
