Do What Matters: Values

~24 minutes Play · sort · reflect Private to you

By the end of this module you can

  • Tell the difference between a value and a goal — and why it matters.
  • Name your top five core values using an interactive card-sort.
  • See how those values give you direction across sport, study, relationships and self.
  • Start living one value on purpose — the E of ACE.
The move Values vs goals Value or goal? Sort your values Across your life The 80th birthday This week

01 · The move

Now — what do you
point yourself toward?

You can now Acknowledge (open up to thoughts and feelings) and Connect (come back to the present). But being open and present isn’t the point on its own — it’s what lets you do the thing that actually matters. That’s Engage, the E of ACE, and it starts with the most important question in the whole program: what do you want to stand for?

The answer is your values — your chosen directions in life. They’re how you want to behave and what you want to stand for, moment to moment. Not rules imposed on you. Not other people’s expectations. Yours.

"Goals are the mountains you climb. Values are the compass in your hand — they point the same way whether you’re at the summit or flat on your back at the bottom."

— Values as direction

Here’s why this matters so much for you, right now. Your teens and twenties are the years you figure out who you are. Chasing goal after goal while ignoring your values is one of the fastest routes to burnout — you can win and still feel empty. But knowing your values gives you a direction that never runs out, in sport and in the much bigger life around it.

02 · The key distinction

A value is not
a goal

This distinction changes everything, so let’s make it crisp. A goal is something you can complete and tick off — win the final, make the team, pass the exam. A value is a direction you can always keep moving in — compete with courage, be a great teammate, keep learning. You never “finish” a value.

A goal…
  • Can be achieved and crossed off
  • Is in the future
  • Is often outside your full control
  • Can leave you empty once you hit it
  • “Win the championship.”
A value…
  • Is a direction, never “done”
  • Is available right now, in this moment
  • Is always within your control
  • Gives meaning whatever the result
  • “Compete with courage and heart.”

Goals still matter — they’re useful mountains to aim at. But when a goal is snatched away (injury, deselection, a bad draw), your values are still completely available. You can play with courage in a final you lose. You can be a great teammate from the bench. That’s why values are the engine of a life that doesn’t collapse when results don’t go your way.

06 · Journal it

The 80th
birthday speech

One of the most powerful ways to find your values is to fast-forward to the end. Picture your 80th birthday (or your retirement from sport). The people who matter most are there, and one of them stands up to say what you stood for and how you showed up. Write slowly — this saves only on this device.

1 · What would you want them to say about the kind of person and competitor you were?

Not your trophies — how you treated people, how you played, what you stood for.

Saved on this device

2 · Where is there a gap between that person and how you’re living right now?

No guilt — just honesty. A gap is simply a direction to walk in.

Saved on this device

3 · Think of someone you admire. What is it really that you admire about them?

Look past what they’ve won — the thing you admire is usually one of your own values talking.

Saved on this device

4 · Pick one of your five values. What is one tiny way you could live it in the next 24 hours?

Small and specific beats big and vague. This is the bridge into Module 06.

Saved on this device

07 · This week’s Mind Gym

Live a value
on purpose

Values only mean anything when you act on them. Values without action are just nice words. Here’s your week: a few ways to bring them to life, and a daily log to keep you honest.

Experiments to try this week

Pick two or three that fit your week and actually run them.

  • On the pitch

    Before each session, choose one value to lead with (e.g. courage, or being a great teammate). Judge the session on that, not the result.

  • Everyday

    Each morning, name the one value you want to bring to the day. Each night, notice one moment you lived it — however small.

  • Decisions

    Facing a tough call? Ask: “which choice moves me toward the person I want to be?” Let the value break the tie.

  • Phone

    Set your top value as your lock-screen or a daily reminder, so it catches your eye when you need it.

  • Relationships

    Pick one person and one value (kindness, honesty, presence) and live it toward them this week — a message, a conversation, showing up.

  • Diary

    Fill in the values log below — one line a day. Watch how often you can act on what matters, even on ordinary days.

The 7-day values log

One entry a day for seven days. Each day, name the value you led with, one action you took to live it, and how it felt. No judgement — some days it’ll be tiny. Everything saves on this device only.

Your values pocket card

Lock it in

You’ve found your compass. You know your five values and how you want to show up across your life. But a compass only helps if you actually start walking. That’s the second half of Engage — Committed Action: turning these values into real, repeatable behaviour, even when it’s uncomfortable. That’s Module 06.
