Do What Matters: Committed Action

~24 minutes Build · design · rehearse Private to you

By the end of this module you can

  • Turn a value into a concrete action with the values-to-action ladder.
  • Design a habit so valued action actually sticks.
  • Use willingness — not motivation — to act even when it’s uncomfortable.
  • Pair action with Acknowledge + Connect — your first full ACE rep.
The move Values → action Design the habit Willingness Reflect This week

01 · The move

Values off the page,
into your life

In Module 05 you found your compass. But a compass only helps if you start walking. Committed action is the second half of Do What Matters: taking your values off the page and turning them into behaviour — again and again, especially when it’s hard.

"Values without action are just nice words. Action without values is just busyness. Put them together and you’ve got a life with direction and traction."

— The heart of committed action

Here’s the trap almost everyone falls into: waiting to feel motivated. Motivation is lovely when it shows up — but it’s a feeling, and feelings are weather. If you only train, study, or reach out when you feel like it, you’ll be at the mercy of your mood. The alternative is willingness: choosing to act because it matters, whether or not you feel like it. And crucially — acting on your values will often trigger exactly the thoughts and feelings you met in Modules 2–4. That’s where all three ACE moves finally come together.

03 · Make it stick · do it now

Design the habit

One action is a start; a habit is what changes your trajectory. The science of habits (James Clear’s four laws) says a behaviour sticks when it’s obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying. Let’s wrap your “today” action in a habit you’ll actually repeat.

1 · ObviousTie it to something you already do, at a set time and place.
2 · AttractivePair it with something you enjoy, or link it to your identity.
3 · EasyShrink it so small it’s almost impossible to say no.
4 · SatisfyingMake it feel good now — tick it off, track the streak.

04 · The engine · do it now

Willingness, not motivation

Here’s the part that ties the whole programme together. The moment you go to act on a value, your mind often serves up the exact stuff from earlier — fear, doubt, “I don’t feel like it,” “what if I fail.” Waiting for that to disappear is a losing game. Instead you use willingness: the choice to make room for the discomfort (Acknowledge), stay present with it (Connect), and take the step anyway (Engage). One full ACE rep.

Waiting for motivation
  • Rarely shows up on the hard days
  • Puts your feelings in charge
  • You act only once you already want to
  • “I’ll do it when I feel ready.”
Using willingness
  • Available in any mood, any moment
  • Puts your values in charge
  • You act because it matters, feeling or not
  • “I don’t feel like it — and I’ll do it anyway.”

Apply in Sport

Choose one values-based training action and design the habit around it this week (you just did — now run it). Show up and do the rep even on the flat days; that’s the whole skill.

Apply in Life

Take one small values-based action outside sport you’ve been avoiding — studying, reaching out to a friend, a hard conversation, a health habit, asking for help. Avoidance is normal; willingness, not motivation, is the engine.

06 · This week’s Mind Gym

Run the rep

This week is simple and hard: do your habit, most days, whether or not you feel like it. Here’s your action to keep in front of you, a few ways to make it easier, and a daily log to build the streak.

Your plan (from above)

Build your ladder and habit above and your plan will show here.

Make the rep easier this week
  • Shrink it

    If you skip a day, don’t scrap it — do the 2-minute version. Showing up small keeps the identity alive: “I’m someone who does this.”

  • Willingness first

    Before the hard rep, say your willingness statement: “I can feel this — and do it anyway.” Then start before your mind can argue.

  • Never miss twice

    One missed day is a blip; two is the start of a new (worse) habit. If you miss, make the very next rep non-negotiable.

  • Pair it

    Attach your action to something you enjoy — a playlist, a coffee, a mate doing it with you. Make it something you look forward to.

  • Diary

    Tick the log below every day you do the rep. The growing streak is the reward — that’s the “satisfying” law in action.

The 7-day action log

One entry a day for seven days. Did you take your action? Log whether you did it, the willingness it took, and one line on how it went. Missing a day isn’t failure — it’s data. Everything saves on this device only.

Your committed-action pocket card

  1. Values without action are just words — climb down to the smallest step.
  2. Design the habit: obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying.
  3. Don’t wait for motivation — use willingness.
  4. Feel the fear and do it anyway. That’s one full ACE rep.

Lock it in

That’s all three moves. You’ve learned to Acknowledge, Connect and Engage — and just now, in the willingness move, you ran them together for the first time. Before we weld them into one fluid skill, the next module goes to the deepest part of the model: the steady, unchanging you that all of this happens within — Self-as-Context.
