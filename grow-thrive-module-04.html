Connect · The second move of ACE

Connect: Be Present

~22 minutes 3 exercises · to actually do Private to you

By the end of this module you can

  • Use your breath to steady yourself on demand with a follow-along pacer.
  • Run the 5-4-3-2-1 "drop anchor" grounding drill under pressure.
  • Build your own visual + physical anchors and a cue word.
  • Experience the observer self — you are the sky, not the weather — the C of ACE.
The move Exercise: breathe Exercise: drop anchor Watch Build your anchors Sky & weather Journal This week

01 · The move

Come back to
right now

You've learned to Acknowledge — to make room for feelings and unhook from thoughts. But you need somewhere steady to do that from. That's Connect: coming back into your body and the present moment. It's the antidote to autopilot, to replaying the past, and to spinning out about the future.

Almost all pressure lives in two places — a mistake behind you, or a result ahead of you. Neither is happening now. The next tackle, the next question, the next breath: that's the only place you can actually perform. Connect is the skill of bringing your attention back there, on purpose, again and again. It has two layers:

Layer 1 · Grounding

Come to your senses

Present-moment contact: drop out of your head and into your body and surroundings — feet on the floor, breath moving, sounds around you. A fast way to steady yourself anywhere.

Layer 2 · The observer

You are the sky

Self-as-context: the deeper, stable "you" that has been noticing thoughts and feelings all along — bigger than any of them, and never damaged by the storm passing through.

"You can't stop the waves, but you can learn to drop anchor in the middle of the storm — and hold steady until it passes."

— The Connect move (after Russ Harris's "dropping anchor")

Enough theory — the whole point of Connect is that you do it. The next three exercises are hands-on. Give each one a real go, right now, wherever you are.

02 · Exercise one · do it now

The breathing pacer

Your breath is the one part of your nervous system you can drive by hand — and a slow, long out-breath is a direct message to your body that it's safe to settle. Pick a pattern, press play, and follow the circle: breathe in as it grows, out as it shrinks. Do at least four rounds.

Follow-along · ~1–2 minutes
Paced Breathing

The numbers are seconds. Settle — in for 4, out for 6; the longer out-breath settles your body, so it’s your go-to before performing. Box — in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4; a steady square rhythm that’s good for focus. Calm — in 4, hold 7, out 8; the deepest brake, best for winding down or before sleep. Don’t worry about being exact — the circle does the counting for you.

Ready
Rounds: 0

Notice what a few slow rounds do — not to your thoughts, but to your body. This is your fastest anchor: you can run three of these before a kick, a serve, an exam, or a hard conversation, and no one will even know.

03 · Exercise two · do it now

Drop anchor: 5-4-3-2-1

When your head is racing, your five senses are always in the present — so they're the fastest way back. This is the classic grounding drill, made interactive: you'll actually look around and notice real things, tapping each one as you go. Do it for real — don't just read it.

Interactive · ~2 minutes
Drop Anchor

First: push your feet into the floor. Straighten your spine, sit or stand tall, and take one slow breath out. Ready? You'll notice things around you and tap each one as you go.

Sense 1 of 5
Notice 5 things you can see
Look around. Tap the button each time you spot one.
You're here. Right now.
That's the whole skill. You just pulled your attention out of your head and into this moment — the only place you can actually do anything. Now pick the one thing that matters next, and turn to it.

You don't always need all five senses — sometimes just feet, breath, and three things you can hear is enough to re-anchor between points or reps. The move is always the same: notice you've drifted, and gently come back.

Count her anchors: a rehearsed countdown, a cue word (“light”), a counting rhythm, a kind voice, a crowd she lets in. Five versions of the same move — attention, brought back on purpose. Your two anchors and cue word from this module are exactly this kit.

04 · Make it yours

Build your two anchors

Elite performers don't ground from scratch each time — they have pre-set anchors they return to. A visual anchor (a fixed point for your eyes) and a physical anchor (a body cue you can feel), plus a short cue word. Set yours now, then write them in your notebook — you'll use them all week.

Set & save
Your Anchors
1 · Your visual anchor — a point your eyes return to
2 · Your physical anchor — something you feel in your body
3 · Your cue word — one word that means "come back"

Your Connect card

When pressure spikes, I fix on [your visual anchor], feel [your physical anchor], and say “[your cue word]”.

05 · The observer self

You are the sky,
not the weather

There's a deeper layer to Connect. All day, thoughts and feelings roll through you like weather — sunshine, cloud, the odd storm. It's easy to believe you are the weather. But notice: there's a part of you that has been watching all of it, calmly, your whole life. That part is like the sky. The weather comes and goes; the sky is never harmed by it, and it's always big enough to hold whatever passes through.

Watch · a moving picture
Sky & Weather

Send the storm across. Watch it arrive, darken things… and pass. The sky doesn't fight it, and the sky isn't damaged. When a result-panic or a wave of self-doubt rolls in, you can practise being the sky: notice the storm, let it move through, and stay steady. This is what protects you when sport gets hard — injury, a bad run, being dropped. You are so much bigger than any single storm.

06 · Journal it

Put it into
your own words

Now slow down and write. This is where the practice becomes insight. Take your time with each — there are no right answers. Write them in your notebook.

In your notebook

1 · Under pressure, where does your attention usually run off to — the past or the future?

A mistake you keep replaying? The result you're scared of? Get specific about your pattern.

In your notebook

2 · Describe a time being fully present made something better.

A moment you were completely in it — a game, a conversation, a place. What was that like?

In your notebook

3 · "You are the sky, not the weather." What does that line mean for you?

Think about your identity beyond results — the you that's still there on the worst day.

In your notebook

4 · Retrieval — in your own words, what three things does Connect do for you?

No looking back. Just what's stuck.

Apply in Sport

Use your two anchors between points, reps or lifts this week. When your focus drifts to the scoreboard, fix your visual anchor, feel your physical anchor, say your cue word — and return to the next job.

Apply in Life

Drop anchor once a day in an ordinary moment — brushing your teeth, waiting for the kettle, before you open your laptop. Ground before sleep, before a presentation, or to break a doom-scroll. Reps in calm build the skill for the storm.

07 · This week's Mind Gym

Practise coming back

Grounding is a muscle — it only fires under pressure if you've done the reps in calm. Here's your week: a few concrete experiments, and a daily anchor log so you can watch the skill build.

Experiments to try this week

Pick two or three that fit your week and actually run them.

  • On the pitch

    Use your two anchors between every point / rep / restart at one training session. Notice how fast you can re-focus.

  • Everyday

    Habit-stack it: every time you wait for a kettle or a red light, run one round of feet–breath–three sounds. Reps off the pitch make it automatic on it.

  • Before sleep

    Two minutes of the Settle breath (in 4, out 6) lying in bed. A long exhale tells your body it's safe to switch off.

  • Phone

    Next doom-scroll, catch it, and drop anchor for 30 seconds instead — feet, breath, look up and name three things you see.

  • Sky & weather

    When a wave of doubt or panic hits, name it as weather: "here's a storm passing" — and be the sky that holds it.

  • Diary

    Fill in the anchor log below — one line a day. Small reps, done often — that’s what builds it.

The 7-day anchor log

One entry a day for seven days. Each day, drop anchor at least once, then log when/where you did it, what you noticed when you came back, and whether it helped. Keep the real log in your notebook — the tracker below is just a helper for this browser.

0 / 7 days

Your Connect pocket card

  1. Notice you've drifted (into the past or the future).
  2. Feet & breath — push your feet down, one slow breath out.
  3. Look up — anchor your eyes, name a few things you can sense.
  4. Cue word — say it, and turn to the very next thing.

Lock it in

Talk it over

Take one of these to a mate, your team, or your coach this week. Saying it out loud is where it gets real.

  • Under pressure, does your attention run to the past (the mistake) or the future (the result)? Can you tell where a teammate’s attention has gone just by watching them?
  • Watch the best player at your next session between plays. What do they do with their eyes, breath and body? Can you spot their anchors?
  • When has being completely present made something ordinary feel great — in or out of sport?

You now have the C of ACE. Acknowledge opens you up; Connect brings you back to a steady present. Together they let you meet any moment without being swept away. Next comes the E — Engage: now that you can be open and present, what do you actually point yourself towards? That's values — the reason any of this matters.
