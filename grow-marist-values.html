YOUR
VALUES

When the game gets hard and confidence is mixed, the question stops being "do I feel good?" and becomes "who do I want to be right now?" That's where your values come in — and they're worth getting clear on.

This is a short, three-part exploration: name the values that matter to you, reflect on the player and person you want to be, and see how rugby fits into the bigger picture of who you are.

Start → 3 parts · about 5 minutes · stays on your device

A value isn't a goal you tick off. "Win the final" is a goal — you either reach it or you don't. "Compete with everything I've got" is a value — you can live it in every single game, win or lose. Goals can be reached; values are a direction you keep walking.

The part of you that notices

You are not just an athlete

There's a part of you that has been there for all of it — your first game, your best game, the one you'd rather forget, every position, every team. The thoughts, the nerves, the form and the slumps all came and went. That part of you noticing it all has stayed the same. That's you — bigger than any single game, label, or result.

Here's why it matters: "rugby player" is something you do, and clearly something you care about deeply. But it isn't the whole of who you are. When your entire identity rests on being a rugby player, a bad game, an injury, or a tough selection can feel like a threat to you. When you hold it a little more lightly — "I'm someone who values courage and connection, and rugby is one of the biggest places I get to live that out" — you can throw yourself completely into the game without it being the only thing holding you up.

Your values travel with you. The courage you show in a tackle is the same courage you can bring to a hard conversation. Sport is one of the biggest arenas where you live your values — but it isn't the only one, and it isn't the source of them. You are.
