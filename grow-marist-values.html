The Mind Gym · A values & goal-setting journey

YOUR
VALUES

When the game gets hard and confidence is mixed, the question stops being "do I feel good?" and becomes "who do I want to be right now?" Your values are the answer to that question — and getting clear on them is some of the most useful work you can do.

This is a guided journey in five short parts. You'll name what matters to you, do a few honest reflections, check how you're tracking, and turn it all into a simple plan you can actually use. It takes about 10 minutes, and nothing is saved — it's just for you.

A goal

A destination

Something you can reach and tick off. Useful — they give your training shape and direction.

For example: make the 1st XV · win the final · hit a new bench PB.

A value

A direction

How you want to play and be, every time you step out — something you can live in any game, win or lose. You never "finish" a value.

For example: compete with everything I've got · back my teammates · stay composed.

Think of values like heading "west." You can always travel further west — you never arrive and stop. Goals are the towns you pass through along the way. Both matter, but the value is the thing that keeps pointing you forward, even after a goal is reached or missed.

Why we do this · from someone who's been there

He won the World Cup —
and woke up empty

Jonny Wilkinson kicked the drop goal that won England the 2003 Rugby World Cup — the absolute pinnacle of the game. And the morning after, he felt completely empty. In this short talk he explains why, and what changed. It's the clearest case we know for doing the work on this page.

Three things to take from it:

1

A goal isn't a value. Jonny had mistaken one outcome — winning — for lasting fulfilment. But goals, once reached, just hand you the next stressful target. Values are a direction you keep walking; they don't run out the morning after.

2

Chasing perfect is a trap. His drive came with a constant "sense of doom" and fear of failure. Knowing what you genuinely value gives you something to move toward — instead of just running away from failing.

3

Pressure lives in the future and the past. He found clarity by letting go of the outcome and staying in the now. Your values do the same job: they tell you who to be right now, whatever the scoreboard says.

That's why we start here. Getting clear on your values isn't about adding pressure — it's about taking it off, so that win or lose, you always know who you're trying to be. Let's find yours.

Part 4 of 5 · Turn it into action

Your game plan

Values point the direction. Committed action is what you actually do to head that way — and the trick is to make it small, controllable, and repeatable. You don't change by thinking about your values; you change by doing one small thing that's in line with them, then doing it again tomorrow.

Aim at the process, not just the outcome

Outcome goals

Not fully yours

Winning, being selected, the scoreline. They matter and they're worth wanting — but you don't fully control them, so they're a shaky thing to hang your week on.

Process goals

Always in your hands

The controllable actions that live out your values — your prep, your effort, your attitude, the extras. Nail these and the outcomes look after themselves more often.

Build your actions

For each value you chose, write one small, controllable action you could do this week that feeds it. Keep it ordinary and specific enough that you'd actually do it — "stay back for ten minutes of extra kicking", not "be more dedicated".

Start here

Your first commitment

Pick the one action that matters most and lock it in. Make it a plan, not a wish — decide the when.

How to run it: aim to do one values-based action a day this week. Notice how you feel during and after — it won't always be "fun", but it should feel meaningful. If you plan seven days and manage five, that's a win. Then come back and go again. That repetition is the training.

Part 5 of 5 · Bring it together

More than a
rugby player

The part of you that notices

You are not just an athlete

There's a part of you that has been there for all of it — your first game, your best game, the one you'd rather forget, every position, every team. The thoughts, the nerves, the form and the slumps all came and went. The part of you noticing it all has stayed the same. That's you — bigger than any single game, label, or result.

Here's why it matters: "rugby player" is something you do, and clearly something you care about deeply. But it isn't the whole of who you are. When your entire identity rests on it, a bad game, an injury, or a tough selection can feel like a threat to you. When you hold it a little more lightly — "I'm someone who values courage and connection, and rugby is one of the biggest places I get to live that out" — you can throw yourself completely into the game without it being the only thing holding you up.

Your values travel with you. The courage you show in a tackle is the same courage you can bring to a hard conversation. Sport is one of the biggest arenas where you live your values — but it isn't the only one, and it isn't the source of them. You are.
